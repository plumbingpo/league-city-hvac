How El Paso Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a system that lasts and one that breaks down in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the first heat wave of the year.

The earlier you report a comfort problem, the cheaper it usually is. A capacitor that's starting to fail is an easy, low-cost part to replace, yet the same part, ignored for a season, can take out the compressor and turn a simple fix into a full system replacement.
